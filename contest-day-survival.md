# Contest Day Survival

*Your first marching contest Saturday, parking lot to midnight*

The band leaves before you wake up, performs for about eight minutes total, and gets home near midnight. Somehow this becomes your favorite day of the fall. Here is the whole day in order, so nothing surprises you but the show.

## The two flavors of Saturday

| | UIL | BOA |
|---|-----|-----|
| What it is | The school league. Region, then Area, then State. | The national circuit. Regionals, Super Regionals, Grand Nationals. |
| How it scores | Region is ratings: earn a Division I and rank high enough to advance. Area is a ranked knockout. State is the Alamodome. | Ranked placements plus captions, prelims and finals at every event. |
| The vibe | Every band in the region, one day, business-like | Bigger productions, longer days, louder crowds |
| Tickets | Gate admission, host sets the price | Real ticket prices, buy ahead for Super Regionals |

Flower Mound runs both worlds, and the November stack is real: BOA San Antonio Super Regional November 6 and 7, then UIL 6A State November 9 to 11, same building, back to back weeks. Book San Antonio hotels absurdly early.

## The shape of the day

1. Your kid leaves early, on the bus, in uniform or hauling it. You are not driving them. Feed them before they go.
2. You leave later, tickets already bought on your phone, and park where the signs say, not where hope says.
3. Prelims: the band performs once in the morning or early afternoon. Eight minutes. Do not be in line for nachos.
4. The wait: hours. The band eats, changes, naps in a gym somewhere. You watch other bands, which is secretly great.
5. Finals announcement: if the band advances, everyone stays. Nobody is going home early. Nobody.
6. Finals at night, under lights, better than prelims every single time.
7. Retreat: all the bands on the field for awards. Scores read from the bottom up. Cardiac event optional.
8. Buses home. Kid walks off the bus at some unholy hour, sunburned and hoarse and happy.

## What to bring

- Stadium seat or cushion. Metal bleachers, ten hours. Do the math.
- Sunscreen and a hat for the day, a layer for the night. Texas does both in one Saturday.
- Phone charger or battery pack. You will film everything.
- Clear bag, because most stadiums enforce it now.
- Cash is mostly useless, gates and concessions run cashless. Cards and phones.
- Binoculars if you like watching your specific kid instead of the show. No judgment.

## Where to sit and how to act

- Sit high, between the 30 yard lines. The show is designed to be read from up there. Front row is for hearing brass and seeing ankles.
- Do not walk in or out while any band performs. Move during the gaps. Ushers at BOA will hold you at the tunnel anyway.
- Clap for every band. Someone's exhausted kid is on that field too.
- When your band takes the field, you may scream. This is Texas. It's expected.

## Following from home

Can't make it? [FloMarching](https://www.flomarching.com) streams BOA and WGI events on a subscription. UIL posts results on [uiltexas.org](https://www.uiltexas.org/music/marching-band), BOA posts on [musicforall.org](https://marching.musicforall.org), and the parent Facebook groups will beat both of them by twenty minutes.

## The ten minutes you get

You will see your kid for about ten minutes, somewhere between the buses and the gate, if you position yourself well. Bring them food they can hold in one hand. Tell them they were great. They were. Then they disappear back into the machine, and you drive home alone listening to the recording you just made.

## Want the good seats? Join the crew

Props and pit crew parents go ON the field. They push the marimbas, set the props, and stand in the end zone while the show happens twenty feet away. It is the best view in the stadium and it costs eight hours of sweat. Cheap. Sign up through the volunteer channels, VOLY background check first.

## After

Scores post late and the internet has opinions. Skip that. Whatever the placement, the kid in your car is exhausted and proud, and the only correct postgame analysis is food. Analysis never, tacos always.
