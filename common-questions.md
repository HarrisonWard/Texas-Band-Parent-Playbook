# Common Questions, Blunt Answers

*Clarifications for new Texas band and guard parents*

Questions we asked, heard asked, or watched get asked in the group chat at 11 p.m. Answers are short on purpose. Long versions live in [the starter guide](new-parent-starter-guide.md).

## Getting in

**Does my kid need band experience to join color guard?**
No. Guard auditions are movement based. Dance and gymnastics kids convert fast, orchestra kids too. Ours went from orchestra to the weapon line.

**We missed spring auditions. Is it over?**
Ask anyway. Programs find spots for motivated kids more often than the posted process suggests.

**What are all these levels?**
Big programs run a JV and varsity marching band and multiple guard levels, placed by audition. Placement is about ability today, not politics. Kids move up.

**My kid is shy and knows nobody.**
Band eats that problem in about two weeks. Sections adopt their freshmen.

## Money

**What does the fee actually cover?**
Usually meals, show shirts, entries, instruction. Usually not shoes, gloves, lessons, trips, merch, or the tea stops.

**Can we pay in installments?**
Yes. Payment plans are standard. Ask.

**We can't afford it.**
Email the director, one line: "we need some help this season." Quiet scholarship processes exist everywhere and they have done it a hundred times. Nobody announces it, nobody judges it.

**Are the fundraisers worth it?**
Yes. The mulch sale (or whatever yours sells) is real money, and some programs credit your kid's account for what they sell.

## Time and camp

**Can we take a vacation during band camp?**
No. The calendar comes out months early, plan around it. Miss camp and someone else learns your kid's dot.

**Why is rehearsal running late again?**
Because posted end times are a suggestion. 20 to 30 minutes over is standard everywhere. Arriving 15 early, though, that one is enforced.

**When does it calm down?**
Fourth Monday of August the 8-hour rule kicks in, then it settles: a couple rehearsals a week, Friday game, some Saturdays.

**Is my kid too tired?**
Probably yes, probably fine. Watch grades and injuries, not tired. Tired is the product. Somehow 500 exhausted kids still put up clean runs every Saturday.

**Can they do band and a sport, or theater, or a job?**
School sports, yes: the handbook says directors and coaches work out conflicts when both sides give a little, and kids do it every year. The fine print that surprises people: CLUB sports and other non-school activities are not excused absences. Neither are club meetings, tutorials, or family outings. Illness and family emergencies are.

**What if my kid wants to quit mid-year?**
Big one, and the policy has teeth: LISD defines band as a full-year commitment, and a student who leaves mid-year sits out a FULL year before returning to the program. So before any October quitting conversation, know that most October misery is fatigue, not fit, and a talk with the directors comes first. The decision point is spring course selection, not the hard week.

**We have a wedding on a band Friday. Now what?**
Absence requests go through the band website form, in advance: 24 hours notice for a rehearsal, two full weeks for a performance. Approval is at the directors' discretion. Unexcused rehearsal absences can cost the halftime performance that week, and unexcused camp absences can move a kid to the Jaguar Band. Ask early, in writing, always.

**How is band graded?**
Four equal parts per nine weeks: pass-offs (playing checkoffs on assigned music), attendance, daily rehearsal etiquette, and performances as major grades. Translation: show up, behave, learn your part, play the shows, and the grade takes care of itself.

## Games and contests

**Do I buy tickets to watch my own kid?**
Yes. Football through the district ticket system, UIL State through Ticketmaster, BOA online or at the gate. Budget for it.

**When do they actually perform at a football game?**
Halftime, plus stand tunes all night. They stay the whole game and get home near midnight.

**What is the difference between UIL and BOA?**
UIL is the school league, everyone does it, ends at the Alamodome. BOA is the national circuit some bands add, also ends at the Alamodome, different weekend. Same field, different scoreboards.

**What does prelims and finals mean for my Saturday?**
They perform in the morning, everyone waits all day, they perform again at night if they advance. Seat cushion, sunscreen, book.

**Can I see my kid at a contest?**
For about 10 minutes while they walk to the bus, if you position yourself well. This is normal and nobody warns you.

**What happens if football makes the playoffs?**
The season stretches. Friday commitments can run deep toward December, the band goes where the team goes.

## Guard

**Is guard part of the band or separate?**
Fall: part of the band, scored inside the band's score, same buses. Winter: its own indoor season (NTCA locally, WGI nationally) with its own fee.

**What is a weapon?**
Rifle and sabre. Wood and plastic, nothing fires, everything gets thrown. It is the advanced equipment, and making the weapon line is a big deal.

**Why does my guard kid have practice before band camp even starts?**
UIL lets guard learn show choreography earlier in the summer than the wind players. Legal, normal, everywhere.

**What is this second fee in November?**
Winter guard. Separate season, separate costume, separate travel. If the guard is good, quietly budget for Dayton in April too.

## School and grades

**Does marching band count for PE credit?**
In Texas, yes, the fall marching semester substitutes for PE credit within state limits. The counselor squares it at schedule time, worth confirming.

**Does No Pass No Play apply to band?**
Yes. Fail a class at a grading period and your kid sits out performances and contests until grades recover under the state rules. Directors run tight ships about grades for exactly this reason.

**A physical, really?**
Really. Entering 9th and 11th grade, plus any 7th or 8th grade marcher, on the UIL form, and a medical history form signed every year. A June appointment beats an August panic.

## Myths that need killing

- "State only happens for our class every other year." Old news. Every classification goes to the Alamodome every year now.
- "The 8-hour rule means only 8 hours of band." Rehearsal is capped. Games, contests, and performance days sit outside the cap.
- "Guard is a side thing." Guard is scored with the band and can decide contests.
- "Band is a fall activity." Band is a school-year activity: marching in fall, concert season and UIL evaluations in spring, winter guard and winter percussion in between.
- "The fee is the whole cost." The fee is the floor, not the ceiling.
- "Communication will be organized because the program is elite." The show will be flawless and the pickup time will still be wrong in three different apps. Unrelated skills.
