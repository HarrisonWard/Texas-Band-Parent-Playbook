# So Your Kid Joined the Band

*A starter guide for new Texas band and color guard parents*

Nobody hands you a manual when your kid comes home signed up for marching band. This is that manual, from a family that went from middle school orchestra straight into a competition band and a world class color guard with zero warning. No speeches about the transformative power of the activity. Just what happens, what it costs, and what you need to do, with sources at the bottom.

Written for Texas. Dates below are for the fall 2026 season. Rules and fees drift, so trust your director's emails over anything on the internet, including this.

## TL;DR

**Buy:**

- Gallon jug
- Athletic shoes, tall socks
- Sunscreen, hat
- Carry-on suitcase with wheels
- Travel power strip
- Multi-port charger (Anker type)
- AirTags for every bag
- Reloadable debit card
- Snack box for the band bag
- One Sharpie, label everything
- The merch
- Weapon kid flying? A long gun case with real locks
- An oil change and tire rotation. You are the logistics department now.

**Expect:**

- Band camp in August heat. Normal.
- Physical on file before they march. Medical history form every year.
- A few hundred to $1,500+ a year. Payment plans exist. Ask.
- Fridays gone. October Saturdays gone.
- Driving 8 hours to a comp and seeing your kid for 10 minutes as they walk to the bus.
- 15 early is on time. Pickup runs 20 to 30 late.
- No single source of truth. Find a veteran parent.
- Bus back at 2 a.m., first period at 8. They survive.
- The 8-hour rule slows it down once school starts. August is the worst of it.
- Feeling lost your whole first semester. Everyone is.
- Volunteer or you will not see your kid until Thanksgiving.

## Band camp, or why your kid smells like that

Texas bands ease in before August 1 because UIL only allows 15 hours of supervised marching fundamentals before that date, and wind players cannot learn the competitive show's choreography yet. On August 1 that restriction lifts and UIL sets no practice limits until the first day of school. That is why early August is wall-to-wall rehearsal. It is not your director freelancing. It is the calendar every band in the state runs.

What your kid actually needs:

- A gallon water jug with their name on it, in Sharpie. This is the one non-negotiable purchase. Skip the stick-on labels, they peel. Sharpie everything, actually.
- Kitchen tip: a pot filler or bathtub faucet fills a gallon jug in seconds. The fridge door will take all morning.
- The band tea stop is a lifestyle. Around here it is HTeaO. Sign up for the rewards program on day one, you are going to fund it either way.
- Athletic shoes and tall socks. Never sandals, never slides, even on "light" days.
- Sunscreen, a hat, sunglasses, and a real breakfast before they leave.
- A box of packable snacks that lives in the band bag and gets restocked weekly.
- Dinner ready and low expectations for conversation afterward.

Programs handle the heat with scheduled water breaks, shade, and indoor blocks. If your kid has a health condition, tell the directors before camp, not after something happens.

One more thing nobody tells orchestra families: big programs field more than one band (JV and varsity) and several guard levels, placed by audition. Prior marching experience is not required for any of it. Kids walk in cold as freshmen and land fine.

## Paperwork before they march

Texas requires a pre-participation physical for marching band, on the UIL's standardized form, upon entering 9th grade and 11th grade, and for any 7th or 8th grader who marches. A medical history form signed by you and your kid is due every single year. Clinics book up in July, so knock this out in June if you can. Your band will send the exact forms and the deadline.

## What it actually costs

Every program posts its own numbers, and they vary a lot by district and by how far the band travels. Ask for the fee sheet before registration day so nothing surprises you. For scale, one large Houston-area program (Cypress Creek) charges $500 for band and $450 for guard in 2026-27, which covers about 15 game-day and contest meals, show shirts, uniforms, entry fees, and instruction.

| Cost | Typical shape | Notes |
|------|---------------|-------|
| Band fee | a few hundred dollars | usually covers meals, shirts, entries, instruction |
| Guard fee | similar, sometimes higher | costumes and equipment are in there |
| Marching shoes | $30 to $60 | plus gloves for most sections |
| Instrument costs | varies widely | school rental fees, reeds, valve oil, sticks |
| Private lessons | $25 to $40 each | optional but common in big programs |
| Trip years | can add $500 to $2,000 | not every year, announced far ahead |

Three things nobody says loudly enough: payment plans are standard, most programs have a quiet hardship process (one email to the director, handled discreetly, they have done it many times), and fundraisers are real money, not busywork.

## Where your weekends went

Friday nights, all season: football. The band plays halftime and the stands. Your kid is there from afternoon call time until close to midnight.

Saturdays from mid-September through early November: contests. Texas has two worlds, and your band may live in one or both.

**UIL** is the school league. Bands advance Region, then Area, then State, at the Alamodome in San Antonio. Every classification now competes at State every year. That changed recently, so veteran parents may still tell you it alternates years. They are out of date, which will delight them. In 2026, conferences 1A/3A/5A go November 2 to 4 and 2A/4A/6A go November 9 to 11.

**BOA (Bands of America)** is a national circuit many Texas bands also enter. In 2026 there are eight Texas regionals from mid-September through late October (McAllen, Bedford, Shenandoah, Iowa Colony, San Marcos, Prosper, Midland, Waco), then the San Antonio Super Regional at the Alamodome on November 6 and 7.

Contest days run long. Prelims in the morning, finals at night if the band advances, home very late. Buy the ticket, bring a stadium seat, and clap for every band. Yes, admission costs money. Welcome to the sport.

## Band time

Call time means 15 minutes early, and the program means it. The posted end time gets no such respect. Expect 20 to 30 minutes past it, most days. Park, queue up a podcast, make peace.

## The 8-hour rule, or why it gets better

Starting the fourth Monday in August (slightly different for early-start districts), UIL caps marching band rehearsal at 8 hours per week outside the school day. Performances and football games do not count against it, and there is an extra hour allowed on performance days. Practical translation: after camp, life settles into a couple of evening or morning rehearsals a week plus Friday games and some Saturdays. The August pace is temporary, by rule.

One asterisk from lived experience: programs use every minute the rule allows, and games, contests, and performance days sit outside the cap. The rule limits rehearsal. It does not limit band.

## If your band travels

Competition bands get on buses. Some get on planes. Field notes from a traveling family:

- Buy a good carry-on size suitcase with wheels. They will drag it across four parking lots in the dark.
- Hotel rooms run four kids deep and outlets are scarce. Send a travel power strip.
- Each kid charges about five things a night (phone, iPad, headphones, battery pack). A multi-port charger, Anker or similar, ends the outlet wars.
- AirTags in every bag. Buses and equipment trucks eat luggage.
- A debit or prepaid card you can reload from your phone beats an envelope of cash.
- They will sleep badly on a bus and perform anyway. I have watched 500 kids too tired to stand walk onto a field and put up a clean run. No mistakes. Nobody can explain it.
- You will drive 8 hours to a contest and see your kid for 10 minutes while they walk to the bus. You will do it again the next weekend, on purpose.
- The bus unloads at the school at 2 a.m. and they go to first period. This is real and somehow survivable.
- Make peace with your kid riding to rehearsals and games with other teenagers. It will simplify your life more than any other single decision.

## Color guard, specifically

- Guard performs with the band in the fall and is scored as part of the band. Same contests, same bus.
- Big programs field multiple guards, sometimes four levels up through world class. Placement is by audition and prior band experience is not required. Dance and gymnastics kids convert fast.
- "Weapon" means rifle and sabre, the equipment that spins and gets thrown. Making the weapon line is a big deal. When your kid says they are a weapon, congratulate them.
- If your weapon kid wants to practice on vacation, you will end up buying an actual long rifle gun case, because nothing else fits a guard rifle. The airline does not care that it is a wooden prop. Expect the full firearm treatment: hard case, real locks, questions at the counter, and a 16 year old learning to check it solo like a seasoned roadie.
- Equipment is flags, rifles, and sabres. The rifle is a wooden or plastic prop. Nothing fires.
- UIL lets guard start show choreography earlier in the summer than wind players, so guard camp may start before band camp. That is legal and normal.
- Costs lean different: costume, gloves, shoes, makeup kit, sometimes hair pieces. The fee sheet will spell it out.
- Many programs also do winter guard, an indoor season from roughly December through April. In North Texas the local circuit is NTCA (North Texas Colorguard Association). Much of the rest of the state runs TCGC (Texas Color Guard Circuit). WGI is the national circuit, and its World Championships run in Dayton, Ohio every April. Separate fee, separate calendar, and if the guard is good, a plane ticket. Ask in the fall whether your program competes in winter so spring does not ambush you.

### What this looks like in practice: Flower Mound

Our program, for scale. Flower Mound HS (Lewisville ISD, 6A) runs around 430 band kids across five concert bands, a varsity Competition Band of roughly 200, and a JV marching band. The guard runs four levels, topping out at world class. The fall resume includes UIL 6A State gold in 2016 and 2025, three BOA Grand Nationals finalist runs, and four BOA San Antonio Super Regional titles. In winter the guards compete in NTCA around DFW and in WGI, whose season ends in Dayton every April.

The point is not to brag on one school. The point is that programs like this exist all over Texas, your kid can walk into one as a freshman with zero marching experience, and the machine knows exactly what to do with them. Ours did.

## How to actually help

- Get on the communication app the first week (Cut Time, BAND, Remind, whatever your program uses), then accept that no single channel has the whole picture. Cross-check the app, the student group chat, and one veteran parent. If you feel lost your first semester, you are on schedule. Everyone is.
- Feed them, hydrate them, wash the show shirt on Thursday nights.
- Volunteer early: props and pit crew (you get on the field), uniforms, meals, chaperoning. Pick one thing per season and do it well. It is also the main way you will actually see your kid between August and November.
- Do not coach from the fence. You will want to. The staff saw it too.
- Do not forget the siblings. A contest is a very long day for a very short show, and nobody has to attend all of them. Let the other kids skip some, and put something on the calendar that is theirs alone.
- Buy the merch. There is a lot of it, it is better than you expect, and showing up in school colors matters to your kid more than they will admit.
- The booster board is regular parents running what amounts to a small company budget, for free, because somebody has to. Go to one meeting before you form opinions. Better, take a small job.
- Start-of-season car care: oil change, tire rotation. You are about to put real miles on dark highways.

## Small glossary

| Word | Meaning |
|------|---------|
| Drill / sets | the choreography of where everyone stands, learned as numbered pages |
| Dot | your kid's exact spot on the field in a given set |
| Pit / front ensemble | the mallet and percussion instruments parked in front of the field |
| Battery | the drumline that marches |
| Drum major | the student conductor on the podium |
| The box | where the judges sit, top of the stands |
| Region / Area | the UIL rounds before State |
| Prelims / finals | morning performance, then an evening performance if the band advances |
| "One more time" | a lie, plan on three |
