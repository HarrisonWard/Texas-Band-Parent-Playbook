# The Instrument Buying Guide

*Rent, buy, upgrade, and the $89 Amazon trap*

At some point someone will suggest your kid needs a better instrument, and you will have no idea if that's true or what it costs. This is the map.

## The ladder, in order

1. **Beginner rental** (middle school): rent from the school's partnered music retailer. Do not buy anything yet, half of sixth grade switches instruments.
2. **School-owned horns**: the big and expensive stuff (tubas, euphoniums, bass clarinets, bassoons, all concert percussion) belongs to the school. At FMHS the maintenance fee is $100 a year, paid by separate check to FMHS Band, and LISD stocks professional-level instruments, so you never buy a tuba. Rejoice. Handbook rules: nobody plays anyone else's instrument, only percussionists touch percussion, and damage beyond normal wear bills to the student.
3. **The mouthpiece upgrade** (worth doing early): $40 to $150, recommended by the private teacher or director, and it improves sound more per dollar than anything else in this guide.
4. **The step-up instrument** (usually late middle school through 10th grade): an intermediate or pro-line horn for a kid who's clearly staying in the game. This is a real purchase, $1,200 to $4,000+ depending on instrument, and it should only happen with the director's or private teacher's specific recommendation of brand and model.

## The rules that save you money

- **Ask the teacher before the store.** Retail commission is real. Your private lesson teacher's used-market tip is usually the best deal in the building.
- **Used is fine, played-first is mandatory.** A used pro horn vetted by the teacher beats a new mid-tier one at the same price. Never buy an instrument nobody you trust has played.
- **The Amazon trap**: the $89 "flute" and $120 "trumpet" in the colorful listings are instrument-shaped objects. Repair shops refuse them because the metal doesn't survive the repair. Directors can spot them from the podium. If a deal looks impossible, it is.
- **Rental credit**: the school-partnered retailers often apply months of rental payments toward a purchase. Ask before you assume renting was sunk cost.
- **Repairs are local, with one rule first**: the handbook says check with a band director BEFORE the repair shop. The district-approved option is Music and Arts at The Shops at Vista Ridge, 500 E. FM 3040, Suite 121, Lewisville. Contest-week emergency repairs are a genre of miracle they perform weekly.
- **Instruments come home every night**: the handbook expects 30 minutes of practice a day and forbids leaving instruments in the band hall overnight. The nightly haul is policy, not your kid forgetting their locker.

## Insure the step-up

Once you own a $2,500 saxophone that rides a school bus, insure it: either a rider on your homeowner's policy or a dedicated musical instrument policy (a modest annual cost, and it covers what school and homeowners policies often exclude, like the bus, the band hall, and gravity). Sharpie the case, AirTag the case, and photograph the serial number today.

## Percussion and guard, the short versions

- **Percussion kids don't buy drums.** The school owns those. They buy sticks, mallets, and a practice pad, roughly a monthly-allowance-sized habit. Marching sticks break, it's normal, buy the multi-pack.
- **Guard kids** get performance equipment through the program, but home practice gear (a practice flag, a practice rifle from a vendor like Designs by King) turns the living room into a rehearsal space. Budget for one lamp. It won't survive. It's a rite of passage.

## What FMHS handles for you

District instrument rentals and their fees run through Presto, uniforms are issued by the program, and the WISK announces retailer nights and repair drives when they happen. When in doubt, one email to the directors before any purchase over $500. They answer this exact email fifty times a year and they would rather you ask.
