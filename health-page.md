# The Health Page

*Heat, hearing, ankles, and sleep. Practical, sourced, zero panic.*

Band is safe, well-supervised, and physically demanding. These are the four things worth actually knowing, plus what changed in Texas this season. This page is information, not medical advice, and your kid's doctor and the school's athletic trainers outrank it.

## Heat, and the new Texas rules

New for 2026-27: UIL made heat safety protocols **mandatory** for all outdoor athletics AND marching band, effective August 1, 2026. Programs now monitor WBGT (wet bulb globe temperature, which factors heat, humidity, wind, and sun) within 15 minutes of practice start and every 30 minutes during, with required work-to-rest ratios and hydration breaks that scale up as readings climb, plus mandatory rapid-cooling zones on site. Translation: the days of "tough it out" are formally over, by rule, statewide.

What you control at home:

- The gallon jug leaves the house full. Every day. Electrolytes on two-a-days help, water alone is fine most days.
- Breakfast with protein before morning rehearsal. Kids who skip it hit the wall by 10am.
- Tell the directors about any condition (asthma, diabetes, POTS, medications that affect heat tolerance) before camp, not after an incident.
- Know the flags: cramps, dizziness, confusion, or a kid who stops sweating in the heat needs staff attention immediately. Programs drill this, kids should still hear it from you once.

## Hearing, the one nobody thinks about

A marching band at full volume runs well past levels hearing experts flag for extended exposure, and the kids standing next to the drumline get the worst of it, every rehearsal, for years. Occupational guidance starts flagging sustained exposure around 85 decibels, and rehearsal peaks blow past that.

The fix costs less than a pair of shoes: **musician's earplugs** (filtered, $15 to $40) drop the volume without muddying the music, which is why pros wear them on every stage. If your kid marches near the battery, plays in the front ensemble, or is IN the battery, put a pair in the band bag and normalize it. Hearing damage is cumulative, invisible, and permanent, and seventeen-year-olds discount all three of those words.

## Guard bodies

Guard is a throwing and catching sport performed on ankles and wrists. The predictable issues are sprains, overuse soreness, and the occasional catch gone wrong. The boring prevention works: shoes that actually fit, conditioning between seasons, taping habits the trainers recommend, and reporting pain when it starts instead of spinning through it for three weeks. "Push through" is a rehearsal ethic, not a medical plan, and the staff would rather hear about a sore wrist Tuesday than lose a performer in October.

## Sleep, the real currency

The season runs on borrowed sleep: early rehearsals, late games, 2am contest returns, first period regardless. Mostly, kids absorb it and are fine. What to actually watch: grades sliding, mood flattening, getting sick every other week. Those are the flags, not tiredness itself, because tiredness is the product.

Help the boring way: protect the no-band nights for actual sleep, feed them real food, and treat the post-contest Sunday as sacred recovery time. Skip the energy drink arms race, and if sleep problems look bigger than schedule math, that's a doctor conversation.

## The safety net that already exists

Texas schools staff athletic trainers who cover band, the new heat rules have teeth, physicals screen for the big stuff, and the medical history form you sign every year is read by real people. The system is built for this. Your job is the jug, the earplugs, the shoes, and the willingness to say something early.
