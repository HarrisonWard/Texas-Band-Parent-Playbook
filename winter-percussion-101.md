# Winter Percussion 101

*The drumline's second season, the loud sibling of winter guard*

If winter guard is guard distilled, winter percussion is the drumline unleashed: battery and front ensemble performing their own complete show on a gym floor, no band attached, judged in the same WGI universe as the guards. Same season, same gyms, more decibels.

## What it is

An indoor percussion ensemble builds a show the way the marching band does, with drill, staging, props, and a soundtrack the pit provides live. Groups compete in classes by skill level, from concert divisions (standstill, pure playing) to movement-based marching classes topping out at world class. The national circuit is **WGI Percussion**, whose championships run in Dayton every April, the week around the guard finals ([dates at wgi.org](https://www.wgi.org/about/future-dates/)). Texas groups also compete in state and local circuit shows through the winter, often sharing Saturday gyms with the winter guards.

## What it looks like at a big program

- Auditions land in late fall as marching season winds down, same window as winter guard.
- Rehearsals run weeknights and weekends, December through April.
- Show days are the same all-day gym Saturdays: warm up in a cafeteria, perform for about seven minutes, awards at night.
- The front ensemble hauls the same instruments as fall plus a full sound system. The equipment logistics are somehow bigger indoors than out.

Whether your program fields a full competitive indoor line every year, and in which circuit, varies by staffing and numbers. At FloMo, the drumline liaison and the WISK are the authority on what this winter holds. The fundraisers you saw in August (that $3,141 recycling haul) are part of what pays for this season.

## What it costs

Same shape as winter guard: a separate winter fee covering the show, instruction, and entries, plus travel if the group hits WGI regionals out of town. Percussion adds equipment wear: heads, sticks, mallets have a season-long appetite. Budget like winter guard, add a sticks line.

## Parent jobs, indoor edition

The floor still gets rolled and taped by parents, and percussion adds the equipment push: racks, speakers, keyboards on carts, all moved into place during a timed transition while the previous group's parents move theirs out. It's a ballet performed by people in matching booster shirts. Sign up, you'll never watch a stage crew the same way again.

## Show day rules

Same as guard: no flash photography, no walking during performances, tight schedules, scores post fast. And the same secret: the small groups from small schools deserve your applause. Every world class line was one of them once.

## Why it matters

Fall makes drummers strong. Winter makes them musicians. The battery that spent October counting steps spends February phrasing, and the improvement compounds straight into next fall's marching show. If your kid lives in the drumline, winter is where they level up. Plan for the fee, enjoy the decibels.
