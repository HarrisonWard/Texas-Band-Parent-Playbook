# The Middle School Pipeline

*For cluster parents of younger kids: how a sixth grader becomes a band kid, and how a freshman with zero experience becomes a weapon*

If you have a younger kid in the Flower Mound cluster, or a middle schooler wondering about high school band, this is how the machine feeds itself.

## The band path

1. **Fifth or sixth grade**: instrument selection. The middle school directors run fitting nights where kids try mouthpieces and get matched to instruments that fit their faces and the band's needs. Let the directors steer, they are building balanced bands years ahead.
2. **Middle school band**: beginner band, then advancing bands, with the [cluster's middle schools](https://fmhs.lisd.net/middle-schools) feeding the FMHS program. The directors coordinate vertically, so what your kid learns in seventh grade is deliberately the on-ramp for ninth.
3. **Eighth grade spring**: the high school band comes recruiting: visits, joint concerts, and registration for the following fall. The physical, the fee, and the calendar from the [starter guide](new-parent-starter-guide.md) all begin here.
4. **FM9**: freshmen are FULL members of the high school marching band while based at the ninth grade campus. Orientation gets coordinated around rehearsal (the WISK literally schedules around it). The kid you drop at FM9 in August performs at the Alamodome by November.

Two accelerators worth the money if you can: private lessons starting in middle school (the single biggest predictor of high school placement), and a decent step-up mouthpiece before a step-up horn (see the [instrument guide](instrument-buying-guide.md)).

## The guard path, which is different and shorter

Here is the secret nobody tells dance moms and gym parents: **color guard has no middle school requirement.** Guard recruits in the spring through clinics and auditions open to anyone, and kids from dance, gymnastics, cheer, and orchestra walk in with zero marching background every year. Movement training converts, fast.

Ours went from middle school orchestra to a world class guard's weapon line in three seasons. That path is open every spring. Watch for audition clinic announcements around March and April, follow [@flomoguard](https://www.instagram.com/flomoguard/), and show up. The guard will teach everything else.

## What to do by grade, the cheat sheet

| Kid's grade | Your move |
|-------------|-----------|
| 5th | Attend instrument selection night, rent don't buy |
| 6th and 7th | Support practice habits, consider lessons, go to their concerts, they notice |
| 8th | Spring: registration, physical, meet the HS program. Summer: relaxed, it's the last quiet one |
| 8th, dancer or gymnast edition | Spring guard clinic. Just try it. The freshman guard class is built from kids who just tried it |
| Rising 9th | Read the [starter guide](new-parent-starter-guide.md), buy the jug, clear late July onward |

## The honest pitch to younger-kid parents

The programs at the top of Texas marching are not assembled in ninth grade, they are grown from sixth. But the door stays open the whole way up: late-starting winds catch up with lessons, and guard rebuilds itself from newcomers every single spring. The machine wants your kid. Feed it.
