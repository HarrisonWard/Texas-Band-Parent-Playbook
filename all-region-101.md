# All-Region 101

*Why your kid is suddenly practicing scales at 6am, explained*

Sometime in September your kid starts disappearing into a practice room with three pieces of music called etudes, and nobody explains what's happening. This is what's happening.

## The short version

Texas runs an individual audition ladder alongside the band season, organized by TMEA (Texas Music Educators Association): **Region, then Area, then All-State**. Kids audition alone, behind a screen, judged blind, ranked against every player of their instrument in the region. The best make the Region band. The best of those advance toward the All-State bands, which perform at the TMEA convention in San Antonio in February. All-State is roughly the top 2 percent of auditioning musicians in the state, and Texas is the hardest state in the country to make it in.

## How the fall actually goes

| When | What |
|------|------|
| Summer | TMEA releases the audition etudes. The grind begins quietly. |
| September and October | Private lessons and practice rooms fill with the same three excerpts on repeat. You will hear them in your sleep. |
| Late November | Region auditions, for FMHS that's **TMEA Region 24**. Blind, ranked. Ninth graders can audition for the 9th grade region band AND the upperclassmen bands. |
| Late January | Region Clinic and Concert: guest conductor, real repertoire, and per the handbook, earning a chair means the full clinic and concert are required, not optional. |
| Early January | The top Region finishers become Area auditionees and travel with other LISD students **to Abilene** for Area auditions. Yes, Abilene. Pack accordingly. |
| February | Top Area finishers are named TMEA All-State and perform at the convention in San Antonio. Resume gold, memory platinum. |

House rules at FMHS, from the handbook: Wind Symphony and Symphonic Band members are **required** to audition for Region. Concert and Varsity Band members are required to learn the music and do the graded pass-offs, and encouraged to audition. Nobody escapes the etudes entirely, which is the point.

## What it costs and what it takes

- A small audition entry fee, plus the region clinic fee if they make it.
- Private lessons are not required but the audition rooms are full of kids who take them. This is where that $25 to $40 a lesson earns its keep.
- No marching required: this is a pure playing contest. Quiet kids with good fundamentals thrive here.

## How to be useful

- Do not ask "did you win." Ask what chair, and then be impressed regardless, because auditioning alone behind a screen at 14 takes nerve most adults do not have.
- The etudes will sound repetitive and occasionally awful for weeks, and then one day they won't. That day is the whole process working.
- If they miss the cut: everyone misses the cut sometimes, the etudes change every year, and sophomores beat their freshman selves constantly. Food, not analysis. It works for auditions too.

## Solo and Ensemble, the spring sibling

The LISD Solo and Ensemble contest runs in **early March at FMHS**, and it's a ratings event, not a ranked one. The handbook makes it real: all wind and percussion students perform a solo AND a chamber ensemble. Solos should be in hand before winter break (suggested lists come home in late November), chamber groups get assigned in December, and accompanied solos require an accompanist, which the band provides at a reasonable fee covering two rehearsals and the performance. Entry fees are on families, financial hardship gets one quiet email to a director, and once entered, finishing is required. It also feeds the [lettering math](lettering.md) directly: First Division ratings are worth real points.
