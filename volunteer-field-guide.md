# The Volunteer Field Guide

*Every band parent job, decoded, so you can pick one on purpose*

Volunteering is not charity here, it is the only reliable way to see your kid between August and Thanksgiving, and the program physically does not run without it. But the jobs are wildly different, and nobody explains that before the signup links fly. Here is the menu.

**First, the prerequisite**: LISD requires an annual background check through the VOLY portal before you help with anything. It takes up to two weeks and commits you to nothing. File it before you need it.

## The jobs

| Job | What you actually do | Time cost | Access level | Best for |
|-----|---------------------|-----------|--------------|----------|
| Props and pit crew | Push marimbas, carry props, build sets, sprint on and off the field during transitions | Every game and contest, plus build days | ON THE FIELD during the show. Best seats in Texas. | Strong backs, dads who miss being on a team |
| Uniforms | Fittings, hemming, game-night checks, hanging 400 uniforms correctly | Big early-season push, then game nights | The band hall's inner sanctum, air conditioned | Detail people, chatters, anyone who can thread a needle or fake it |
| Meals and feeding | Away game meals, contest meals, fruit break during camp | Bursts around events | The cafeteria command center | Kitchen generals, Costco black belts |
| Chaperones | Ride the buses, count heads, hold the medical bag, herd with love | Full game or contest days | Everything the kids see, you see | Calm people, nurses, natural den parents |
| Hydration crew | Water on the field at rehearsals and games | Weekly, short shifts | Sideline adjacent | Anyone with a truck and a hose reel |
| Photography team | Shoot rehearsals and events, upload to SmugMug | Flexible, self-directed | Field edge with a camera | Camera owners who were looking for an excuse |
| Merch table | Sell at previews, games, and events | Event windows | The social crossroads of the program | Retail veterans, people who like cash boxes |
| Floor crew (winter) | Roll, stretch, tape, and strike the winter guard floor in under two minutes | Winter Saturdays | On the gym floor at every show | Guard parents, adrenaline enjoyers |
| Committee and board | Budgets, contracts, fundraising, the machine behind the machine | Real hours, year round | How it all works | Spreadsheet people, future presidents |

## How to start without overcommitting

1. Do the VOLY check now.
2. Pick ONE job for the fall. One. The program will happily absorb your whole life if you let it, and the veterans would rather have one reliable shift than five flaky ones.
3. Signups come through SignUpGenius links in the WISK and the parent Facebook groups. The good shifts go fast after each WISK lands on Sunday night.
4. Show up early once and you're family. The uniform moms remember everything.

## An honest ranking of the perks

1. **Pit crew**: you stand on the field during the show. Nothing else comes close.
2. **Chaperone**: you hear the bus culture, which is where band actually happens.
3. **Uniforms**: air conditioning and the best gossip.
4. **Meals**: 400 teenagers say thank you to you specifically.
5. **Floor crew**: winter's version of pit crew, plus you learn to move like a stagehand.
6. **Board**: no perks, just the quiet knowledge that none of it happens without you. Someone's got to, and the ones who do are the best of us.
